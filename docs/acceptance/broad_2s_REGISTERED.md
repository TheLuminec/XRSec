# Broad 2 s programme: what the ball-throwing result depends on (registered 2026-10-01, before any run)

The ball-throwing result (`ballthrowing_cross_day_REGISTERED.md`, Result) rests on one model family: the
Nymeria treatment retrained at 2 s, `dyn`. The user asked for broader tests overnight. This programme asks
four questions of that result, all at 2 s so they can be scored on single throws.

Every arm except one changes exactly one thing against the treatment_2s arm (same seeds 1-3, same 48
held-out Nymeria users, same split resolved through the loader's own `select_validation_users`, 3,072
training identities, generated by `treatment_short_lists.py`). The exception is the control arm, whose
composition is the 10 s control's.

| arm | config | changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| treatment_2s (exists) | `--seed s` | nothing (reference) |
| **control_2s** | `--seed s --arm control` | Nymeria training users dropped; BOXRR+alyx only, the 10 s control's 1,024 validation users |
| **treatment_2s_raw** | `--seed s --encoding raw` | encoding `raw`: absolute position and orientation kept |
| **treatment_2s_br** | `--seed s --encoding br` | encoding `br`: pose relative to the window's first frame, so absolute pitch and roll are removed |

**Scoring.** Every gated checkpoint is scored with `ballthrowing_cross_day.py` on cuda (gate 1e-4). Every
`dyn` checkpoint (control_2s plus the existing treatment_2s) is also scored with `alyx_cross_day.py` on cuda.
Reading is `broad_2s_read.py`, committed with this file and fixture-tested before any result exists.
Ball-throwing differences are per user (N=41), averaged over the three seeds and then bootstrapped over users
(`exposure_breadth_read.ci`). Alyx uses (seed, user) units, paired across window lengths on the same units.

**Resolution.** Ball-throwing paired intervals between arms of different composition have run at a half-width
of about 0.04 (the day and headset costs read ±0.04). An effect under about 0.04 will therefore land as "not
resolved" or as spanning two regions, and is reported that way.

All tables below partition the line. Each region's meaning is written before the run.

## Q1. Does Nymeria training matter on an unseen cross-day corpus?

Row `nym_C1`: treatment_2s minus control_2s, C1 (same headset, other day), per user, seed-paired.

| region | meaning |
| --- | --- |
| below 0 | FALSIFIER: Nymeria training does not help cross-day identification on an unseen corpus |
| 0 to +0.02 | not resolved: nil or too small to see |
| **+0.02 to +0.10** | **BAND**: daily-life AR-glasses training carries to a third, unseen activity, at the size it carried to Questset (+0.027) or somewhat larger |
| +0.10 and above | exceeds: report as such |

Row `nym_persistence`: (C1 - C0) for the treatment minus (C1 - C0) for the control. This is the analogue of
alyx's "breadth does not change persistence".

| region | meaning |
| --- | --- |
| below -0.03 | Nymeria training makes identification less persistent across days |
| **-0.03 to +0.03** | **BAND**: no change in persistence. Nymeria moves the level, not the day cost |
| above +0.03 | Nymeria training makes identification more persistent |

## Q2. The static-cue audit on ball-throwing (`raw` against `dyn`)

The project's standing rule is that every headline gets its static-cue audit beside it. Ball-throwing is an
unseen corpus, so the normaliser fits once over the whole corpus, every headset pooled, and each headset's
scene-origin offset stays in. The raw model can read where a person stood and how tall they are.

The prediction is that a static cue adds within a headset and is scrambled across headsets, because the
tracking frame changes with the headset.

Row `raw_C1`: raw minus dyn, C1.

| region | meaning |
| --- | --- |
| below 0 | raw does not add across days within a headset: no persistent static cue the model can use here |
| 0 to +0.05 | small |
| **+0.05 and above** | **BAND**: a static cue adds across days within a headset. It is read as height and placement, not as behaviour, and is reported beside the `dyn` headline, never in place of it |

Row `raw_headset`: the headset cost (C2 - C1) under raw, minus the same under dyn.

| region | meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **below -0.05** | **BAND**: a headset change scrambles the static frame raw relies on, so raw pays more for it than dyn |
| -0.05 to 0 | small: raw pays somewhat more, within resolution |
| 0 and above | FALSIFIER: raw survives a headset change at least as well as dyn. The static cue here is not frame-bound, and that would need explaining before any raw figure is quoted |

Raw at 10 s selected epoch 1 on Across-XR. Record `best_epoch`; an epoch-1 selection is expected and is not
by itself a defect.

## Q3. Does absolute tilt carry headset fit? (`br` against `dyn`, plus a training-free lookup)

`dyn` keeps gravity, so absolute pitch and roll stay in. One untested account of the headset cost (-0.235)
is that how a headset sits on a person's head shifts their tilt, which `dyn` then reads as identity.

**Q3a, model.** `br` removes absolute orientation by expressing each frame relative to the window's first
frame. The quantity read is the persistence ratio rho = mean C2 / mean C1, compared paired on the same 41
users. dyn's rho is 0.458 / 0.693 = 0.661. A ratio is used rather than a difference because `br` is
expected to sit lower overall, and a lower level shrinks absolute costs mechanically.

**Precondition.** br's C1 interval lower edge must be at least 0.15, about six times chance. Otherwise the
row is unreadable, a ratio of near-chance numbers, and is reported so.

Row `br_rho`: rho(br) minus rho(dyn), with a paired user bootstrap of the ratio of means.

| region | meaning |
| --- | --- |
| below -0.05 | FALSIFIER: removing absolute tilt does not reduce the proportional headset cost. The tilt account is not supported |
| -0.05 to +0.10 | not resolved |
| **+0.10 and above** | **BAND**: consistent with tilt carrying headset fit |

**Confound, stated now.** `br` differs from `dyn` in more than tilt. Its reference is the first frame rather
than the mean pose, and its position is rotated into that frame. So a BAND result reads "consistent with",
never "shown", and the training-free lookup below is the direct half.

**Q3b, training-free tilt lookup (AVALON, CPU, no model).** For each throw window, take the mean of the
world up-vector expressed in the device frame. Its x and z components are roll and pitch. Standardise per
session type over the 41 people; as the height lookup does, this removes each headset's common offset.
Then score rank-1 at N=41 by Euclidean distance, gallery = session mean, probe = each throw, with the
harness's `rank <= 1` rule, for C1 and C2.

**Gate.** The same code path, run on head height, must reproduce the recorded `C1_height` and `C2_height`
per user from `ballthrowing_cross_day_s1.json` exactly. Height is checkpoint-independent, so this gates the
plumbing.

**Precondition.** Tilt C1's lower edge must be at least 0.049, twice chance. Otherwise tilt carries no
persistent identity and cannot explain anything.

Row `tilt_headset`: tilt C2 minus tilt C1.

| region | meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **below -0.05** | **BAND**: tilt identity is headset-bound, as the fit account requires |
| -0.05 to 0 | weak |
| 0 and above | FALSIFIER: tilt survives a headset change as well as a day, so it cannot account for the headset cost |

Q3b runs on AVALON tonight. Its result is committed before any br checkpoint is read.

## Q4. Is ball-throwing's smaller day cost a window-length effect?

Ball-throwing paid -0.131 across days at 2 s, and alyx paid -0.261 at 10 s. Those two numbers differ in
corpus **and** window length. Scoring treatment_2s on alyx cross-day holds the model family fixed and
changes only the window length. The quantity read is the persistence ratio cross-day / same-day:

- alyx at 10 s: 0.483 / 0.743 = 0.650;
- ball-throwing at 2 s: 0.693 / 0.824 = 0.841.

**Precondition.** The 2 s same-day interval's lower edge must be at least 0.15. Otherwise the row is
unreadable.

Row `alyx_rho_2s`: the alyx persistence ratio at 2 s, with a (seed, user) unit bootstrap.

| region | meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **0.75 and below** | **BAND**: alyx stays costly at 2 s, so ball-throwing's smaller day cost is a property of the corpus or task, not of window length |
| 0.75 to 0.84 | partly window length |
| 0.84 and above | FALSIFIER: at 2 s alyx persists as well as ball-throwing does. The half-size day cost is a window-length effect and must not be read as a corpus difference |

Also reported, descriptively: control_2s on alyx (the 2 s analogue of the treatment against control
contrast), and the paired 2 s minus 10 s difference in alyx cost on the same units.

## Result, Q3b (AVALON, 2026-10-01): tilt carries identity across days, and partly loses it across headsets

Artefact `ballthrowing_tilt_lookup.json`, committed before it was read. Reading `broad_2s_read_tilt.json`.

**Gate.** The lookup's code path, run on head height, reproduces the recorded `C1_height` and `C2_height` for
all 41 users. The gap is exactly 0 on both its own path and the harness's.

| tilt lookup, rank-1 N=41 (chance 0.024) | value |
| --- | --- |
| C1, same headset, other day | **0.152** [lower edge above 0.049, so the precondition is met] |
| C2, other headset, other day | 0.096 |
| `tilt_headset` (C2 - C1) | **-0.055 [-0.101, -0.014]** |
| roll only / pitch only, C1 (descriptive) | 0.080 / 0.071 |

**Verdict.**
- The interval spans BAND and "weak". The mean sits in BAND.
- The falsifier is excluded: the whole interval lies below zero.

**Reading.**
- Mean head tilt is a persistent cue on its own, at about 6 times chance across days and above the
  height lookup (0.10).
- A headset change costs it about a third of its value. So tilt identity is at least partly
  headset-bound, as the fit account requires.
- The account is supported, not shown. The model-side test is Q3a (`br`).

**Scale.** Even if tilt explained all of the model's headset cost, tilt alone would lose 0.055 on a cue worth
0.15. The model loses 0.235. So tilt can be at most part of the headset cost unless the model reads tilt
far more sharply than a two-number lookup does.

**Descriptive.** Each headset has its own common pitch offset: Quest about -8 degrees, Vive about -6, and
Cosmos about -1. The per-session-type standardisation removes it. Under `dyn`, which keeps gravity, a model
sees it, but the offset is common to everyone on a headset, so it carries no identity by itself.
